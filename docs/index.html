Abstract

Scene understanding is essential for applications such as indoor robotics, but training such systems requires large-scale annotated datasets, which are expensive to obtain. Generating realistic synthetic data provides a promising alternative for scalable dataset augmentation. In this project, we propose CloudNet, a diffusion-based framework for style-controllable indoor scene image generation. Given a text prompt, a semantic mask, and a style reference image with an associated style prompt, the model generates images that preserve the structural layout while allowing flexible control over visual style. This enables the creation of diverse training data without additional manual annotation, as the generated images inherit the semantic masks (annotations) from real images.

The proposed method combines a Stable Diffusion backbone with ControlNet for structural conditioning and a Style IP-Adapter for style control. The Style IP-Adapter extends the original IP-Adapter by introducing a learnable style embedding, enabling both instance-level (reference image-based) and abstract style guidance.

The proposed CloudNet is then compared with FreestyleNet, which is also capable of generating images with different styles. Experimental results show that while FreestyleNet achieves stronger text alignment, CloudNet performs better in terms of structural consistency and style quality, as measured by Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) and CLIP-Aesthetic scores. These findings demonstrate the effectiveness of combining structural and style conditioning in diffusion models. Future work will focus on improving the generalisation ability of the style module to support unseen styles without relying on predefined styles.

The approach

Layout meets
visual style.

CloudNet combines Stable Diffusion v1.5 with an indoor ControlNet for semantic layout conditioning and a Style IP-Adapter for visual guidance.

The adapter retains four reference-image tokens and adds one learned style token. Only the style embedding and its projection are optimized during Style IP-Adapter training.

04 / Ablation study

What does the style token add?

Controlled adapter comparison

Original IP-Adapter and Style IP-Adapter share the trained ControlNet, 100 denoising steps, and an IP-Adapter scale of 0.8. The Style IP-Adapter adds the learned style embedding module.

Adapter
AdapterCLIP-Text ↑CLIP-Image ↑CLIP-AES ↑mIoU ↑
Original IP-Adapter24.95380.82766.029329.33
Style IP-Adapter (CloudNet)24.82860.83596.148728.98

Adding the learned style token raises overall reference similarity and aesthetic score, with small decreases in text alignment and mIoU. The improvement is specific to visual guidance rather than uniform across all metrics.

05 / Model selection

Why checkpoint 6500?

Training steps, not inference steps

The final model prioritizes visual style quality. Among the evaluated checkpoints, 6500 has the highest CLIP-AES score and a CLIP-Image score close to the best result.

Checkpoint 500 achieves the highest mIoU and CLIP-Text. Choosing 6500 therefore reflects the project's visual-quality objective, rather than an improvement on every metric.
